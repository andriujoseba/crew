## Round economics

Twenty-eight PRs created in the window reached panel review. Across those PRs,
the API records **128 blocking verdicts**: **106 approvals** and **22 change
requests**. Their timelines contain **150 review-request events** and **40
round-answered signals**. These totals are derived by selecting
`APPROVED|CHANGES_REQUESTED` from `/pulls/<n>/reviews`, `review_requested` from
`/issues/<n>/timeline`, and comments beginning `📣 round answered at head` from
the same timeline for PRs #245, #250, #251, #254, #257, #258, #260, #262,
#263, #266, #270, #273–#278, #281, #282, #287, #288, #292, #293, #300,
#305, #307, and #309.

Every one of the 128 verdicts has a preceding request for the same reviewer.
Pairing each verdict to that reviewer's latest preceding `review_requested`
event gives a **median request-to-verdict latency of 11m 22s**, a **mean of
13m 7s**, a **90th percentile of 20m 40s**, and a **maximum of 40m 51s**.
These figures are derived by converting the paired event timestamps to epoch
seconds, sorting all 128 deltas, and taking the nearest-rank 50th and 90th
percentiles. They measure reviewer response after dispatch, not queueing before
the engine issued a request.

PR #281 is the clearest waste register. At unchanged head
`18f787e5e3ed970688761cda36a0e58e3ba8d49c`, review requests were posted at
10:35:18/19, 10:45:12, 10:55:12, and 11:05:13 after the 10:08:12 signal. The
head accumulated seven change-request verdicts and one approval; repeated
change requests landed at 10:44:20, 10:50:21, and 11:00:21 after the initial
round. The loop ended only when `danmt` converted the PR to draft at 11:27:39.
Sources: `/issues/281/timeline` joined by head and actor to
`/pulls/281/reviews`.

End-to-end round cost remains to be derived per signal, head, request, and final
verdict; the aggregate above does not pretend that a raw request event is a
distinct round.

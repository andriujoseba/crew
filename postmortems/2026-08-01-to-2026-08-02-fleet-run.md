## Round economics

Twenty-seven PRs created in the window reached panel review. Across those PRs,
the API records **128 blocking verdicts**: **106 approvals** and **22 change
requests**. Their timelines contain **150 review-request events** and **40
round-answered signals**. These totals are derived by selecting
`APPROVED|CHANGES_REQUESTED` from `/pulls/<n>/reviews`, `review_requested` from
`/issues/<n>/timeline`, and comments beginning `📣 round answered at head` from
the same timeline for PRs #245, #250, #251, #254, #257, #258, #260, #262,
#263, #266, #270, #273–#278, #281, #282, #287, #288, #292, #293, #300,
#305, #307, and #309.

Every one of the 128 verdicts has a preceding request for the same reviewer.
Pairing each verdict to that reviewer's latest preceding `review_requested`
event gives a **median request-to-verdict latency of 11m 22s**, a **mean of
13m 7s**, a **90th percentile of 20m 40s**, and a **maximum of 40m 51s**.
These figures are derived by converting the paired event timestamps to epoch
seconds, sorting all 128 deltas, and taking the nearest-rank 50th and 90th
percentiles. They measure reviewer response after dispatch, not queueing before
the engine issued a request.

PR #281 is the clearest waste register. At unchanged head
`18f787e5e3ed970688761cda36a0e58e3ba8d49c`, review requests were posted at
10:35:18/19, 10:45:12, 10:55:12, and 11:05:13 after the 10:08:12 signal. The
head accumulated seven change-request verdicts and one approval; repeated
change requests landed at 10:44:20, 10:50:21, and 11:00:21 after the initial
round. The loop ended only when `danmt` converted the PR to draft at 11:27:39.
Sources: `/issues/281/timeline` joined by head and actor to
`/pulls/281/reviews`.

For round cost, a completed round is a signal-delimited interval containing at
least one opinionated verdict; signal retries that drew no verdict are not
counted as review rounds. There were **35 completed rounds** across the 27 PRs:
**19** ended with three verdicts, **11** with four, **three** with six, and
**two** with two. The median signal-to-final-verdict duration was **27m 33s**,
the mean **31m 38s**, and the maximum **1h 55m 54s** (#307). These numbers come
from sorting each PR's `📣` comments, assigning requests and reviews before the
next signal, and subtracting the signal timestamp from the last opinionated
review timestamp. They include dispatch delay and reviewer work, so they are
the end-to-end cost of the protocol rather than reviewer response time alone.

## The board versus the truth

The 27 reviewed PR timelines contain **311** `state:*` or `blocker:*` label
writes: 157 additions and 154 removals. The dominant cycle was 39 additions of
`state:addressing`, 39 of `blocker:unrequested`, and 36 of
`state:bots-reviewing`; the derivation selects `labeled|unlabeled` events whose
label begins with either prefix from the same timeline corpus used above.

PR #281 is the measured truth gap. `state:bots-reviewing` stood from 10:20:16
to 11:26:33 (**1h 6m 17s**). The first round had already produced one approval
and two change requests by 10:35:14, but the unchanged-head signal was reused
to dispatch five further requests through 11:05:13. Thus the board said the
panel owned the move while the engine was manufacturing new panel work from a
closed round; neither `state:addressing` nor `blocker:unrequested` appeared
until 51m 19s after that first complete verdict set. Sources:
`/issues/281/timeline` and `/pulls/281/reviews`, joined on reviewer and head.

The durable board record cannot measure notification yield honestly. GitHub
records the `attention` label and comments, but `notify.log` records which
notices were emitted, suppressed, or acted on; that comparison remains in the
box-corpus section rather than substituting label writes for notifications.

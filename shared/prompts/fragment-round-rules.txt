Fix-round protocol, three hard rules: (1) ROUND PICKUP IS ANNOUNCED — the moment you pick up a completed review round on your PR, before touching any code, post one comment: '{{MARK_ADDRESSING}} <sha>' followed by a round analysis covering every blocking and non-blocking point from every reviewer, each marked agree / disagree / needs-ruling plus concretely how you will address it; this is your plan of record, so reviewers can object early if you misread a point. (2) COMMIT CADENCE — never more than 15 minutes between pushes while you are working; if 15 minutes pass, push whatever exists (WIP commits are explicitly fine; never rewrite pushed history mid-round — reviewers track head SHAs); if the tree is genuinely untouched because you are reading, thinking, or stuck, update your worklog comment instead with what you are doing and why there is no commit. Silence is the only violation: a stall must produce evidence of the stall. (3) THE WORKLOG LIVES THROUGH FIX ROUNDS — keep the '## Worklog' checkbox discipline going during round fixes exactly as during builds: add the round's fix steps as checkboxes to the PR body, check off and push as you complete them, and re-request the reviewers when the fix is done AND the check at your head is green. RE-REQUEST BY HEAD, NOT BY VERDICT: an approval is of a specific head, so if answering the round pushed any commit, EVERY panelist is re-requested — including the ones who already approved — because their approvals are now stale and the handoff predicate counts only approvals at the current head; a panelist you leave un-re-requested after a push can never approve the tree you actually shipped, and the PR stalls looking finished. Only when the head did not move (you answered with argument or evidence and pushed nothing) do you re-request just the non-approvers. A REVIEW REQUEST REQUIRES A GREEN CHECK AT THE HEAD: if the check is red, that is your next task and not the panel's — fix it and push. Never open a round on a red head; a reviewer's first act is to read the check, and a red one costs you the round and every finding that round would otherwise have made. GREEN IS A RULED TERM ({{DOCTRINE_BUILDER}}, operator 2026-07-27): a cancelled or stale check is NOT a green head — the rollup is scoped to your current head, so what survives there is same-head cancellation, not supersession by a newer push — while a skipped or neutral one IS green, because those are deliberate 'passed / not applicable' conclusions. NOT-YET-FINISHED IS NOT GREEN EITHER: a queued or running check has not answered the question the panel will ask, so WAIT for it to settle and then act on what it settles to — this is the case that bites when you answer a round with argument and push nothing, because then no new run is coming and the check you re-requested under is the one that later fails. The single exception is a repo with NO checks configured at all: there is nothing to wait for, so request and say so. Waiting costs you one tick; re-requesting under an unsettled check costs the whole panel a round. Your own green local run is evidence about your machine; the check at the head is the shared artifact the panel will actually read. The one exception is a failure genuinely outside the PR — a runner outage, a flaky dependency, a failure already present on the default branch — and it is an exception only if the request SAYS SO EXPLICITLY and names the evidence (for example: the same job fails identically on origin/main at <sha>). Silence about a red check is the thing prohibited; an argued exception is fine. THE PANEL IS THE PR REPO'S ROSTER MINUS YOU: read the panel= line in the repo's .github/labels.conf — it governs over any prose or remembered list. If the repo names no panel= roster, ask triage on the authorizing issue before marking ready-for-review — do not guess ({{DOCTRINE_BUILDER}}). For orientation, the fleet bench is: {{BENCH}}. Never request review from {{TRIAGE}}: triage rules on issues and answers questions, it does not vote on PRs, and a review request to it sits outstanding forever, making the PR look permanently short of a full round. When you need triage's attention, @-mention {{TRIAGE}} in a comment — that is the channel for rulings, not a review request. The same roster rule governs any panel list a PR itself AUTHORS: if your build writes a panel list (a labels.conf panel= line, a CONTRIBUTING roster), write the current bench, never port a repo's stale pre-ceremony list — cast#143 shipped a kimi-less panel exactly that way, and the handoff then read the roster the PR had authored and handed off a reviewer short.
